How memory actually works
Passive re-reading feels productive but barely sticks. Real retention comes from active recall (trying to remember before peeking) and spaced repetition (returning after time has passed). The stages below escalate from full visibility to total recall — each round is a workout for your memory.

Read with intention.

Slow down. Read aloud in your own voice. Notice the rhythm, the stresses, the meaning. Click any word to hear the passage from there.

One chunk at a time.

The mind holds about seven items at once. Drill each sentence until it feels effortless, then stack the next one on top.

The first-letter method.

Used by actors, preachers, and competitive memorizers. Each first letter is enough of a prompt for your mind to retrieve the whole word.

Fade to blank.

As you grow more confident, dim the text. Hover over any word to bring it back. Push the slider until the page is nearly empty — that’s mastery.

The final test.

A note on rhythm
After a focused session, walk away. Sleep is when memories consolidate. Return tomorrow and recite again — that single act of remembering after a delay does more than an hour of fresh study.
